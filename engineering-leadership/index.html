Engineering leadership · beyond the résumé

How I think about building software and leading teams.

I've worked across very different company sizes, organizational structures and disciplines. That range shaped a view of engineering leadership that starts with people and outcomes rather than with technology or org charts.

Software should adapt to the people using it whenever possible—not require people to understand and conform to the internal model of the software.
Scale & ambiguity

Large matrixed organizations and small startups

I've operated in environments where progress requires alignment across large organizations, shared platforms and many dependencies, and in smaller environments where roles blur, ambiguity is high and you have to personally move the problem forward. I value being able to change how I operate without changing the underlying principles.

Cross-functional perspective

I've lived inside technology, product and design organizations

I've been hired into and worked as part of each of those disciplines. At eBay, for example, I led a technology services group inside a large design organization. I don't see engineering as a downstream implementation function. Good product development happens when product, design and engineering shape the problem together and share responsibility for the outcome.

Design thinking

Understand the user before optimizing the system

I'm a strong proponent of design thinking, user research and direct observation. Before automating a workflow or designing an architecture, I want to understand what the person is actually trying to accomplish, where the friction is, and which complexity belongs in the system instead of in the user's head.

Technical depth

I can build technically. Technology still isn't the point.

I can work deeply enough with architecture, data, platforms, integrations and application development to make technical decisions and build working software. But technical sophistication is useful only when it helps solve the right problem. I am much more interested in whether a system makes someone's work better than whether it uses an impressive stack.

Systems thinking

Product, architecture, process and organization interact

Many hard engineering problems aren't purely technical. Ownership, incentives, workflow, system boundaries, communication and architecture reinforce each other. I tend to look for the underlying system rather than treating each symptom as an isolated problem.

Leadership

Give teams problems and outcomes, not just tickets

I prefer teams with clear ownership, enough customer and business context to make good decisions, and meaningful responsibility for outcomes. Leadership should create clarity and constraints without removing the team's ability to think.

A useful example · eBay

Largo — prototyping and testing for the buyer experience

At eBay I led a technology services group within a large design organization. We built Largo, a prototyping and testing platform for the eBay marketplace's buyer experience. That structure put engineering directly inside the design practice: technology wasn't simply receiving finished designs to implement; it was helping designers and product teams explore, prototype and test experiences as part of the design process itself.

The current projects are part of the same story.

The scale is smaller, but the principles are visible in the work elsewhere in this collection. The Service Intake project begins with the mechanic's workflow rather than with a database schema. The Test Ride app puts a narrow staff experience over CRM and waiver systems rather than forcing a salesperson to operate those systems directly. The Max's transformation work starts by observing the real operation before deciding what should be automated, integrated or left alone.

Those projects let me work unusually close to the user again—and make the connection between engineering leadership, product thinking, design and operations very explicit.
