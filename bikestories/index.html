Product / design exploration

BikeStories

A living history for bikes people actually care about — photos, specifications, documents, modifications, components, memories and ride stories organized around the bicycle rather than scattered across apps and folders.

The owner shouldn't have to maintain a database to tell the story of a bike.

Capture

  • Photos and documents
  • Specifications and components
  • Modifications and maintenance
  • Ride stories and memories

Agent-assisted organization

An agent could ingest the raw material, identify useful structure, and help build the bike's history instead of forcing the owner through a large manual form.

Living bike page

A coherent view of what the bike is, how it changed, what happened to it, and why its owner cares about it.

Share & repurpose

  • Public/shareable bike story
  • Social posts
  • Blog/story output
  • Detailed sale listing
Design questions

Still intentionally unresolved

  • What should the owner library feel like?
  • What makes a “bike story” different from an inventory record?
  • What deserves prominence: the object, its components, its timeline, or its stories?
  • How little structured input can we require from a non-technical owner?
  • What makes someone want to return and keep the story alive?

Status: concept/design exploration. Implementation is intentionally parked while the product experience is explored.
