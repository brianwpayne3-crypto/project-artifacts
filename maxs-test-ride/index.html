Test Ride App

A focused staff workflow for turning a signed waiver into useful customer and test-ride context without requiring shop staff to operate the CRM directly.

1. Customer signsSmartwaiver captures the rider and waiver event.
2. Customer resolvesHubSpot contact is safely found, created or updated.
3. Staff selectsRecent signers surface immediately; search remains available.
4. Ride recordedBikes, notes and buying-journey outcome are captured.
5. History persistsStaff can see prior rides and CRM context on return visits.

The problem

A general CRM contains the customer, but it is not the natural interface for a salesperson standing beside an e-bike and a customer who just signed a waiver. The app narrows the interaction to the actual job.

Outcome, not “follow up?”

Instead of asking staff to predict whether follow-up is needed, each ride records where the customer actually is: Bought Today, Very Interested, Still Researching, or Not Interested.

Identity is harder than email

A real shared-household-email production bug exposed the danger of treating email as person identity. Matching rules were refined so distinct riders are not silently overwritten simply because contact data overlaps.

Shopping parties

The model is evolving to recognize that people may shop together while remaining independent customers with independent outcomes. One person buying should not erase the other person's sales journey.

Sessions, not form saves

The emerging analytics model treats a customer visit as a Test Ride Session rather than counting individual notes or bikes as separate visits. Simplicity for staff matters more than manufacturing false precision.

Reporting belongs with the domain

The app should eventually answer what happened to the people put on bikes: riders, outcomes, models ridden, visits before purchase and conversion—without forcing staff into HubSpot reporting.

System boundary

Smartwaiver → HubSpot customer identity ↔ Test Ride App → Test Ride domain / analytics

HubSpot owns customers. Smartwaiver owns signed waivers. Test Rides owns test rides.

Architecture direction

The first version uses HubSpot heavily for persistence and history. The planned evolution introduces PostgreSQL/Supabase for structured Test Ride sessions, bikes ridden, outcomes and analytics while continuing to reference HubSpot contact IDs. This is intentionally not an attempt to rebuild HubSpot as a custom CRM.

Product principle

Commodity systems can remain underneath the workflow while the employee sees a small interface designed around the task in front of them.
