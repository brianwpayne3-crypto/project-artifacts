Operator-led transformation · Max's Electric Bikes

Improving the business by working inside it.

A small-business transformation effort spanning customer experience, service operations, CRM, waivers, commerce, inventory and purpose-built staff tools. The work starts with how people actually operate, then decides whether to simplify an existing platform, connect systems, or build something narrow.

The user shouldn't have to understand the architecture of the software to run the business.

Customer & test rides

Replaced disconnected/manual customer handling with a focused Test Ride workflow connected to Smartwaiver and HubSpot, while keeping customer identity in the CRM and the staff experience in a purpose-built interface.

Waiver operations

Moved from WaiverMaster toward Smartwaiver, with signed-waiver events feeding HubSpot and the Test Ride workflow. Repeat signing, identity matching and shared-email edge cases became product problems rather than invisible staff friction.

AIM → Square

Migration strategy deliberately avoids recreating the legacy POS. Square becomes the transaction platform; only business capabilities that actually matter are migrated. Native Square is preferred before custom software is considered.

Catalog & inventory

Rather than importing unreliable legacy inventory, current physical bikes were recaptured into a clean operational baseline and modeled around real manufacturer variations. Inventory scope is added only where tracking creates value.

Physical bike lifecycle

Receiving, serial capture, assembly, customer orders, accessories and readiness are being treated as a lifecycle of a specific physical bike—not as isolated checklists or fields scattered across systems.

Customer obligations

Special orders, parts still owed, bikes awaiting assembly and customer follow-up are modeled as obligations the business must not forget simply because a transaction or bike handoff occurred.

Architecture principle

Use the platform where it fits. Hide it where it doesn't.

For each workflow: use the SaaS platform directly when its native workflow is genuinely simple; put a narrow interface over it when the capability is right but the staff UX is wrong; build a Max's-specific capability only when the platform does not model the need cleanly.

This work is the operating context from which the longer-term Manager AI concept emerged: once operational systems contain trustworthy state and obligations, an AI layer can help an owner understand what requires attention without forcing them to continuously inspect every underlying system.

Current direction

Not a custom ERP.

The goal is a coherent operation, not maximum custom software. HubSpot owns CRM/customer identity. Smartwaiver owns signed waiver documents. Square owns transactions and commerce where appropriate. Narrow applications own specialized workflows such as Test Rides. Custom operational data is introduced only where the business needs a model the SaaS systems do not provide.
